# Proxy evaluation on the DASS dataset

Generated by `python evaluation/run_proxy_eval.py` (from `backend/`). Do not edit by hand; rerun the script instead. Raw rows: `results/proxy_eval.csv`.

**What this measures:** agreement between SurveyDoctor's careless-response indices and a validity proxy (claiming to know at least one of three made-up words). Claiming fake words measures overclaiming, which overlaps with careless responding but is not the same thing. These numbers are agreement with a proxy, not accuracy.

## Setup

- Data: `data/raw/dass/DASS_data_21.02.19/data.csv` (openpsychometrics.org DASS dataset, 2017–2019), 39775 rows; 39775 respondents analysed.
- Proxy: 5192 respondents (13.1%) claimed at least one fake word (VCL6, VCL9, VCL12). This base rate is the chance level for PR-AUC.
- Scales: Depression, Anxiety and Stress, 14 items each, 1–4 answers, no reverse items (item mapping from the official DASS scoring template).
- Longstring is computed in the order each respondent saw the items. The survey showed the 42 items in a different random order to each person (columns Q#I).
- Response time: the sum of the per-item times; median 202 s for all 42 items.

## Limitations

- The proxy is imperfect in both directions: a careful respondent can overclaim knowing a word, and a careless respondent can leave the fake words unticked. The checklist also came after the DASS items, so it captures attention at a later point.
- The dataset only contains people who said their answers were accurate and could be used for research, so the most openly careless respondents are already excluded.
- Even-odd consistency rests on only 3 scales per respondent here, so it is noisy.
- Results are for one survey (42 items, 4-point scale, self-selected online sample).
